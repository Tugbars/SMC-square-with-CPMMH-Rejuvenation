Weight Accumulation in SMC²: Diagnosis, Analysis, and Mitigation Strategies for Long Time Series

Technical Report
January 2026

Abstract

We document a critical failure mode in Sequential Monte Carlo Squared (SMC²) algorithms when applied to long time series (T > 500): catastrophic weight accumulation leading to population collapse. While SMC² performs excellently on shorter sequences, the multiplicative nature of importance weights causes the effective sample size to degenerate irreversibly at scale. We diagnose the root cause, demonstrate that standard rejuvenation via Particle MCMC is insufficient, and propose a practical mitigation strategy combining weight reset (SMC² with refresh) and adaptive hyperparameters. Our CUDA implementation achieves stable inference on T=1000+ observations where vanilla SMC² fails. We provide empirical evidence, theoretical context from the literature, and concrete implementation guidance.

1. Introduction

SMC² (Chopin et al., 2013) is an elegant algorithm for online Bayesian parameter estimation in state-space models. It maintains a population of θ-particles, each carrying its own particle filter over latent states. The method combines the sequential nature of particle filtering with the rejuvenation power of Particle MCMC, promising the best of both worlds.

In practice, SMC² works beautifully on moderate-length sequences. Our CUDA implementation achieves accurate posterior inference with T=500 observations in ~50ms. However, a troubling pattern emerges: as T increases, accuracy degrades catastrophically, even as the algorithm reports successful completion.

This report documents our investigation into this failure mode. We found that the root cause is not a bug, but rather a fundamental limitation of the vanilla SMC² design when faced with cumulative likelihood computation over long horizons.

2. The Problem: Weight Accumulation

2.1 Mathematical Formulation

In SMC², each θ-particle maintains a weight proportional to its cumulative marginal likelihood:

log wθ(T) = Σt=1T log p(yt | y1:t-1, θ) (1)

This is a sum of T terms. Even for well-specified models with good parameters, different θ values produce slightly different log-likelihoods at each timestep. Over T steps, these differences accumulate:

T Best θ log-lik Worst θ log-lik Spread (log) Weight Ratio
100 -150 -180 30 1013
500 -750 -900 150 1065
1000 -1500 -1800 300 10130
2000 -3000 -3600 600 10260

A weight ratio of 10260 means one particle has essentially all the probability mass, while the other 255 particles are numerically zero. This is weight degeneracy.

Figure 1. Population collapse in vanilla SMC². Starting from a diverse population, successive resample-rejuvenate cycles progressively reduce diversity. By t=1000, all particles are clones of a single ancestor, resulting in catastrophic underestimation of posterior uncertainty.

2.3 Why Rejuvenation Fails

The standard SMC² response to weight degeneracy is the rejuvenation step: after resampling, run K steps of Particle MCMC (PMMH or CPMMH) to diversify the cloned particles. In theory, this should restore population diversity. In practice, it fails for three reasons:

Key Insight #1: Rejuvenation Starts from Clones

After resampling 256 particles with highly skewed weights, you have ~256 copies of ~10 distinct θ values. Rejuvenation runs K independent MCMC chains starting from these clones. With K=5 steps and ~30% acceptance rate, roughly 70% of particles remain exact clones. The chains haven't had time to explore different regions of parameter space.

❌ Insufficient Rejuvenation (K=1)

With 1 MH step and 30% acceptance, 70% of particles are still exact clones. The population hasn't diversified at all.

❌ More Steps Don't Help (K=50)

Even with K=50, the chains explore locally around their starting points. They don't discover distant modes that were eliminated during resampling.

2.4 Contrast with Standalone CPMMH

An important observation motivated our investigation: a standalone CPMMH sampler (single chain, 3000+ iterations) achieves excellent parameter recovery on T=2000 data, while SMC² collapses. Both methods use the same likelihood estimator. Why does one succeed and the other fail?

✓ Standalone CPMMH

  • Single chain, no weights
  • No accumulation—each proposal judged independently
  • Bad regions get rejected; chain stays at good value
  • Can't "collapse"—just slow mixing

✗ SMC² (Vanilla)

  • Population with multiplicative weights
  • Weights accumulate over T: O(T) divergence
  • Bad θ gets resampled away permanently
  • Population collapses to clones

3. Diagnosis: Identifying the Root Cause

3.1 Initial Hypothesis: Krejuv Too Low

Our first hypothesis was simple: with K=1 (one MH step per rejuvenation), we weren't diversifying enough. We increased K progressively:

Krejuv Params within 15% Acceptance Rate Verdict
1 3/8 32% Poor
5 3/8 38% Poor
10 4/8 35% Marginal
50 4/8 33% No improvement

Result: Increasing K beyond 5 provided no benefit. The problem isn't insufficient MCMC steps—it's something more fundamental.

3.2 The Real Issue: Weight Reset

The literature (Chopin 2002, Del Moral et al. 2006) pointed us to the answer: after resample + MCMC moves, the particles are approximate posterior samples. Their weights should be uniform, not cumulative likelihoods.

Key Insight #2: Information is in Particle Values, Not Weights

After rejuvenation, the evidence from y1:t is encoded in:

  • Which particles survived resampling (selection pressure)
  • Where MCMC moved them (posterior exploration)

Continuing to accumulate likelihood on top of this double-counts the evidence. The weights should be reset to uniform after rejuvenation.

4. The Solution: SMC² with Refresh

4.1 Algorithm Modification

The fix is surprisingly simple. After the resample-rejuvenate step completes, reset all weights to uniform: 

// After resample + K MCMC steps:
kernel_reset_outer_weights<<<grid, block>>>(particles, N_theta);

__global__ void kernel_reset_outer_weights(ThetaParticlesSoA particles, int N_theta) {
    int idx = blockIdx.x * blockDim.x + threadIdx.x;
    if (idx >= N_theta) return;
    
    particles.log_weight[idx] = 0.0f;                    // log(1) = 0
    particles.weight[idx] = 1.0f / (float)N_theta;       // Uniform
    particles.log_likelihood[idx] = 0.0f;                // Fresh accumulator
}

This transforms SMC² from "one long run with accumulating weights" to "a sequence of fresh SMC blocks connected by particle values."

4.2 Theoretical Justification

This approach is well-established in the literature under different names:

  • IBIS (Chopin, 2002): Iterated Batch Importance Sampling with resample-move
  • SMC² with refresh (Chopin & Papaspiliopoulos, 2020)
  • Resample-move SMC with weight reset (Gilks & Berzuini, 2001)

The theoretical point: MCMC moves transform importance-weighted particles into unweighted samples. If you keep the old weights, you're mixing two incompatible representations.

5. Additional Optimizations

5.1 Reducing Resample Frequency

Each resample-reset cycle discards information. We can reduce the frequency by lowering the ESS threshold: 

state->ess_threshold_outer = 0.25f;  // Was 0.5, resample less often
ESS Threshold Resamples (T=1000) Params within 15%
0.5 13 3/8
0.3 4 6/8
0.25 3 7/8

5.2 Increasing Population Size

More θ-particles provide better posterior approximation and slower ESS decay: 

SMC2StateCUDA* state = smc2_cuda_alloc(512, 256);  // N_theta=512 instead of 256

With Nθ=512 and ESS threshold 0.25, we achieved 7/8 parameters within 15% relative error at T=1000.

5.3 Proposal Tuning

The optimal MH proposal standard deviation is approximately 0.5–1.0× the posterior standard deviation (Roberts & Rosenthal). We observed our proposals were too conservative:

Parameter Proposal σ Posterior σ Ratio
mu_base 0.10 0.32 0.31 (too small)
mu_rate 0.15 0.46 0.32 (too small)

Doubling proposals for slow-mixing parameters improved acceptance rates and posterior accuracy.

7. Remaining Challenges and Future Work

7.1 The σz Problem

One parameter, σz (innovation noise in the latent regime process), consistently shows ~40% relative error even with all fixes applied. This is likely due to weak identifiability in the model—the likelihood surface has a ridge where different combinations of ρ and σz produce similar observations.

7.2 Principled Adaptive Proposals

Our current proposal standard deviations are hand-tuned. The literature suggests using the empirical covariance of the current θ-particle population:

Σproposal = (2.38² / d) · Cov(θparticles) + ε·I (2)

This would make the algorithm fully adaptive with no tuning parameters.

7.3 Rolling Window / Block SMC

For very long sequences (T > 10,000), even with weight reset, information accumulates across the entire history. A rolling window approach that only conditions on the most recent L observations may be necessary.

8. Conclusion

We have documented a fundamental limitation of vanilla SMC² for long time series and demonstrated a practical solution. The key insights are:

  1. Weight accumulation is inherent to importance sampling over long horizons—it's not a bug, it's a feature that becomes pathological.
  2. Rejuvenation alone cannot fix collapsed populations—K MH steps from clones still produces clones.
  3. Weight reset after rejuvenation is theoretically justified and practically essential for T > 500.
  4. Hyperparameter tuning matters—ESS threshold, population size, and proposal scales all interact.

Our CUDA implementation with these fixes achieves stable, accurate inference on T=1000+ observations in under 100ms, making online Bayesian parameter estimation practical for real-time trading applications.
